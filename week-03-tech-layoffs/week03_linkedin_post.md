## Post body

Everyone reads a layoff announcement the same way: "that company must be in trouble."

I analysed 4,418 tech layoffs from 2020 to 2026. The data says we've been reading them wrong.

Here's what 6 years of layoff records actually show 👇

1️⃣ Layoffs aren't a recession story.

The COVID year (2020) cut ~81k jobs. 2021 went almost silent — just 16k. Then the real wave hit: 165k in 2022, a record 264k in 2023, and it never stopped. 2026 is already at 114k in under 5 months — on pace to break the record. The single worst month in 6 years? January 2023 — a year of economic recovery.

2️⃣ The biggest layoffs come from the healthiest companies.

Mature, public companies are just 24% of layoff events — but 63% of all jobs cut. The 12 companies behind the largest cuts: Amazon, Intel, Meta, Oracle, Microsoft, Dell, Cisco, Salesforce, Tesla, Google. Not one struggling startup. And the more a company had raised, the bigger its cuts.

3️⃣ "Layoff" means two opposite things.

A mature public company cuts a median 10% of staff — a trim. A seed-stage startup cuts a median 100% — it shuts down. Same word; one is a haircut, the other a death certificate.

So — is a layoff a sign of failure?

For the giants that generate the headlines, increasingly no. Two-thirds of all cuts came from profitable public companies trimming ~10% of staff — an efficiency move, and in 2025-26 increasingly an AI-restructuring one. The companies truly in trouble are the small startups, and when they "lay off," it's usually everyone.

This was Week 3 of my weekly data-science portfolio — a public EDA on a topic everyone argues about, with a data-backed answer.

## Numbers reference (so you can answer questions confidently)

**Dataset:** 4,418 layoff events · 2,913 companies · 66 countries · 11 Mar 2020 – 21 May 2026
**Total disclosed job cuts:** 918,178 (a floor — headcount is undisclosed for ~34% of events)

**Jobs cut by year:** 2020 = 80,998 · 2021 = 15,823 · 2022 = 165,269 · 2023 = 264,320 (record) · 2024 = 152,922 · 2025 = 124,636 · 2026 = 114,210 (141 days → ~296k annualised)

**Worst months:** Jan 2023 = 89,709 · Nov 2022 = 53,594 · Mar 2026 = 46,536

**By stage:** Post-IPO = 1,053 events (23.8%) and 580,961 jobs cut (63.3%). Median % of staff cut: Post-IPO 10%, Seed 100%. Median event size: Post-IPO 200 people, all other stages 70.

**Funding vs layoff size (median people cut):** <$50M = 44 · $50–200M = 60 · $200M–$1B = 100 · >$1B = 250

**Top companies (total jobs cut):** Amazon 58,124 · Intel 43,115 · Meta 35,700 · Oracle 31,294 · Microsoft 30,055 · Dell 23,650 · Cisco 18,521 · Salesforce 16,525 · Tesla 14,500 · Google 13,697

**Shutdowns:** 348 companies laid off 100% of staff.
**AI as a layoff category:** 0% of events in 2020–21 → ~3.9% in 2025–26.
